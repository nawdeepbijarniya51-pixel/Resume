02 / How it was measured

Evaluation protocol

The supplied evaluator reconstructed the trained networks and used forward passes only. It did not alter the checkpoint.

Validate

All 18 expected generator and discriminator arrays were present, finite, and had the expected dimensions.

Sample

With NumPy seed 20261119, 1,000 real rows were sampled without replacement from the provided Fashion-MNIST CSV and 1,000 latent vectors were generated.

Compare

Discriminator scores, threshold accuracy, pixel statistics, pairwise pixel-space distances, sample grids, and latent traversals were inspected.

Pixel values were normalized using pixel / 127.5 - 1. The generator used LeakyReLU (slope 0.2), row-wise layer normalization (epsilon 1e-8), and tanh output; the discriminator used sigmoid output.

Distribution summary

The generator learned a subset of the visual structure

Generated images show clothing-like global shapes, but their average per-pixel variability and pairwise distances are lower than the real sample set in this 1,000-image comparison. A domain-trained classifier and feature-space coverage analysis would be a stronger way to assess category diversity.

05 / Training context

Losses are a coupled signal

The supplied summary describes changing adversarial losses; they should not be read like a single model’s validation loss.

Reported loss movement

Generator loss moved from 3.267 to 3.037, with a minimum near 1.820. Its final value is below the initial value but above its minimum, so it did not improve monotonically. Discriminator loss moved from 0.423 to 0.471.

The modest discriminator-loss increase could indicate generated samples became somewhat harder to classify during training. Yet the post-training scores show the discriminator still separates the evaluated groups strongly.

What this does—and doesn’t—say

GAN objectives are coupled: each model changes the task faced by the other. Loss scales depend on the exact objective and implementation; neither a low value nor a trend alone proves convergence.

The full epoch-by-epoch log and fixed-noise sample grids across epochs would be needed to assess training stability and dynamics.

06 / Interpretation and limits

What the evaluation can claim

Keep the empirical observations separate from what was not measured.

Discriminator accuracy is not a beauty score

The 90.45% balanced accuracy says this trained discriminator can distinguish these real and generated samples at the chosen threshold. It is data- and domain-dependent and does not directly measure image quality.

No FID was reported

Standard Inception FID is not a good feature-space match for 28 × 28 grayscale Fashion-MNIST. No fixed, pretrained Fashion-MNIST feature extractor was supplied, so feature-based FID was not computed. A raw-pixel Fréchet calculation produced a singular-matrix warning and was correctly excluded; raw-pixel distance would not be FID.

Training-domain caveat

The evaluation reads 1,000 examples from the supplied Fashion-MNIST training CSV. The training script reads an IDX image file. Verify that the two inputs match in dataset, preprocessing, and split before interpreting the comparison as a held-out result.

Checkpoint epoch is uncertain

The training loop saves at epoch indices 0, 5, …, 95 for a 100-epoch run. A file named gan_weights_99.npz is not produced by that save condition unless it was saved separately or renamed. Valid tensor shapes do not prove which epoch the checkpoint represents.

07 / Overall assessment

Partial learning, with a quality gap

The checkpoint generates recognizable coarse Fashion-MNIST-like clothing shapes, indicating that the generator learned meaningful global structure. Samples still show noise, blur, repeated patterns, and lower measured pixel-space diversity than the real comparison set. The discriminator is substantially better than chance at separating generated from provided real samples.

The evidence supports partial learning with a generator–discriminator gap and possible partial mode concentration. It does not support a claim of high-fidelity generation or complete mode collapse.
